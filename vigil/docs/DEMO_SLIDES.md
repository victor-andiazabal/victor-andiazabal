# Space Vigil demo, 7 slides

Space Apps accepts a project demo of up to 7 slides or a 30-second video. The deck has 8 slides; drop slide 6
("Built for the whole mission") if the 7-slide limit applies to your submission. The finished deck is
`VIGIL-demo.pptx` in this folder, with a PDF export beside it. The design follows the Slidesgo "Earth's
Atmosphere Class" look: dark sky, atmosphere layers, orbit rings, condensed caps headings. Fonts are Bebas Neue
(headings) and Barlow (body), both free on Google Fonts; install them before opening the .pptx in PowerPoint, or
use the PDF. `SpaceVigil-how-it-works.pptx` is a two-slide companion on data collection and the dashboard. Below is the outline: each slide has a
headline, the visual, and two or three lines of speaker text.

---

## Slide 1. Space Vigil

**Visual:** app logo on the dark dashboard, team name, challenge name.

Crew health self-assessment and decision support for long-duration missions.
Les Tigres de Bethesda. NASA Space Apps 2026, Create Health Monitoring Software for Astronauts on Space Missions.

---

## Slide 2. 24 minutes to Mars

**Visual:** simple diagram, Earth and Mars with the one-way delay; NASA's five hazards as five icons.

On a Mars transit a question to the flight surgeon takes up to 48 minutes round trip.
NASA's Human Research Program names five hazards; each shows up first as a small shift in an everyday number.
Crews need software that notices those shifts and says what to do.

---

## Slide 3. One screen, one answer

**Visual:** dashboard screenshot, Commander on mission day 30, "Act now" ring, 14 colour-coded tiles.

Fourteen indicators from the challenge brief: blood pressure, heart rate, glucose, temperature, sleep, mood,
plus SpO2, mass, exercise, stress, reaction time and radiation dose.
Green, amber, red at a glance. Each tile shows today's value and the person's own baseline.

---

## Slide 4. Judged against you, not the average

**Visual:** sleep trend chart with the baseline band, launch line, scenario window and the 7 h reference line.

Fourteen pre-flight days give each astronaut a median and a robust spread per indicator.
A resting heart rate of 95 is normal for most adults and abnormal for someone whose baseline is 55.
Published cut-offs (ACC/AHA, CDC, ADA, NASA-STD-3001) are drawn on the same chart.

---

## Slide 5. Every alert explains itself

**Visual:** alert card: "Reaction time well below your baseline", Why, Do, source links, checklist open.

Every alert states the rule, the numbers, the published source, and a checklist.
A one-click message to the flight surgeon bundles the day's values and flags, so one exchange is enough.
The reaction self-test is a 60-second form of the PVT-B that ISS crews already run.

---

## Slide 6. Built for the whole mission

**Visual:** settings screen: four environments, four scenarios, mission-day slider; phone screenshot beside it.

Station, transit, rover or base: comm delay and expected daily dose come from measured values.
Scenarios: solar particle event, sleep debt, early infection. Replay any mission day.
Offline, on-device, exportable. Works on a tablet or phone in the cabin.

---

## Slide 7. What is real and what is next

**Visual:** source table from the Learn screen; short roadmap list.

All thresholds trace to NASA, peer-reviewed or clinical sources; crew data is simulated and labelled.
Next: wearable and cuff integration over Bluetooth, flight-surgeon side view, OSDR-derived baselines,
validation of the short reaction test against the 3-minute PVT-B.
